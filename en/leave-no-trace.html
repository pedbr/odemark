Leave no trace

Nobody should see you were there.

Ödemark points at ground without human traces. That only works if every visit leaves it unchanged. Seven principles — built on Leave No Trace, adapted to Swedish terrain and allemansrätten's core rule: do not disturb, do not destroy.

1 · Plan at home

Most damage happens unplanned: darkness arrives, rain arrives, and camp lands where it shouldn't. Planning is the protection. Check weather, fire bans and the zone's local rules before you leave. Keep a fallback a kilometre away. The computation in the app is the planning — the rest is packing: a stove, a waste bag, a trowel.

2 · Camp on durable ground

Rock slab, gravel, sand and dry needle-mat tolerate a tent. Moss, reindeer lichen and wetland do not — reindeer lichen can take thirty years to grow back. Use ground that is already resilient rather than wearing new ground down. One night on one spot, then move on: grass and undergrowth recover from one night, not from three.

3 · Carry everything out

Everything you carry in, you carry out. The organic too: an orange peel lasts two years, a cigarette butt ten. Food scraps draw animals to the spot — the next tent gets the visit. Toilet: dig 15–20 cm deep, at least 50 metres from water, cover thoroughly. Paper is carried out or buried as deep. Wet wipes are always carried out; they are plastic.

4 · Leave what you find

Stones, lichen, antlers, cultural remains — they stay. Protected plants are not touched; what is protected varies by county. Build nothing: no branch beds, no lean-tos, no cairns. A cairn says "someone was here" — the opposite of what both you and the next person came for.

5 · Treat fire as borrowed ground

A stove first — that is why the app shows fire bans as a separate data layer. If you do make fire: only on durable ground, never on a rock slab — it cracks, and the scar is permanent. Use an existing fire ring if there is one. Dead wood from the ground, never broken from trees, never sawn from standing snags — they are homes. Extinguish with water until the ash is cold to the touch, scatter it, and restore the ground.

6 · Respect the wildlife

You are a guest in their living room. Keep your distance, especially during nesting and calving, spring and early summer. Dogs under strict control 1 March – 20 August — in practice, leashed. Never feed wildlife; keep food sealed. If the same bird warns you twice, the tent is in the wrong place: move it.

7 · Be considerate

Of people too. Don't camp within sight or earshot of someone else's camp if the terrain allows otherwise. Sound carries far over water. Greet landowners; respect worked land and fences — close gates. And don't share exact places publicly: a published spot stops being the spot you found. The app is built on the same principle.

The app sends you where nobody has been. The next person should be able to get the same answer.
